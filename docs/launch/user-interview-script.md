**Goal.** Find out whether small-business people would use Verd.io every month. Not whether they like it. Talk to 5 to 10 people.

**Files you need.** The demo (no sign-in) and the messy file from [bake-off.md](bake-off.md) saved as `messy-sales.csv`. Use the messy file, not the person's own data, unless they offer. If they bring their own file, tell them it is read in the browser and that you will not copy it.

---

## Rules for you, the interviewer

- Ask open questions. Do not explain the product before the task.
- Never say "great" or "good" about their answers. Say "thanks, that helps".
- Do not defend Verd.io. If something is confusing, write it down.
- Silence is fine. Count to five before you speak.
- Past behaviour is worth more than future promises. Ask "when did you last..." more than "would you...".
- Do not mention a price until the pricing questions.

## Before the call (1 minute)

- Ask permission to take notes (and to record, if you want to). Say what you will do with them. Say they can stop at any time.
- Say: "I'm testing an idea, not selling. There are no wrong answers. You won't hurt my feelings."

## Part 1. Screening (2 minutes)

Use these at recruitment or at the start. Stop the interview politely if they fail the first two. Record the answers either way.

1. Do you run, or help run, a business with a few staff to about fifty?
2. Does it sell products, or services you can count in orders or invoices?
3. Do you keep sales or stock numbers in a spreadsheet, or export them from a tool (a shop platform, a till, accounting software)?
4. Do you look at those numbers yourself, rather than only an accountant doing it?
5. Are you, or do you work for, someone who sells analytics or business software? (If yes, note it. Their answers will differ.)

## Part 2. Their world (3 minutes)

1. Tell me about the last time you looked at your sales numbers. What were you trying to find out?
2. How did you get from the data to an answer? What tools did you use?
3. How often do you do that? Monthly? Weekly? Only when something goes wrong?
4. What is the most annoying part?
5. Has a number ever been wrong, or surprised you, in a way that cost you time or money? What happened?

## Part 3. Task with the demo and the messy file (5 minutes)

Read this out: "I'll ask you to do three things. Please say aloud what you are thinking. I'm testing the tool, not you."

**Task A (demo, 2 minutes).** Open the demo. "Imagine this is your business. Find out what the biggest risk is, and decide what you would do about it this week."

Watch: where they click first, what they read, what they ignore, what they say.

**Task B (messy file, 3 minutes).** "Now upload this file from a shop. Find the total sales, and tell me whether you trust it."

Watch (do not hint):
- Did they find the sales total?
- Did they notice the note about rows left out, returns, a calculated value, or a row with no date?
- Did they check the number against anything?
- What did they do when something was unclear?

Probe, after they finish:
- "What do you think the tool did with the rows it couldn't use?" (Note whether they know.)
- "If you had to defend this number to your bank or accountant, what would you check first?"

## Part 4. Trust (2 minutes)

Do not lead. Do not say "do you trust it".

1. In your own words, what is this tool doing with your file?
2. Where do you think your data goes? (Write down what they say. Do not correct yet.)
3. What would you need to see or know before you used it with your real numbers?
4. Who else would need to be okay with it, such as a partner, an accountant or a customer?
5. When a tool gives you a forecast, what do you do with it?

## Part 5. Value and pricing (2 minutes)

1. What do you use today for this, and what does it cost you in money and time per month?
2. If this didn't exist tomorrow, what would you do instead?
3. What would you expect something like this to cost? (Wait for a number. Then ask: "What makes you say that?")
4. At what monthly price would you start to think it is too expensive to bother with?
5. At what price would you start to doubt it is any good?

Do not name a price yourself.

## Part 6. Close (0 to 1 minute, fits in the rest)

1. "Would you use it next month, with next month's file? Why or why not?"
2. "Can I put a time in the diary a month from now for you to try it again?" (A booked slot is a stronger sign than a yes.)
3. "Who else should I talk to?"

Thank them. [THANK-YOU GIFT, if any.]

## How to read the results

Decide before you start what would count as a yes. A suggestion:

- **Go:** at least 4 of 8 people say they would use it monthly **and** at least 3 book a second session.
- **Fix the first thing:** if most people could not find the total or did not notice what was left out, fix that before more interviews.
- **Stop and rethink:** if people like it but nobody has a monthly habit with their numbers today, the monthly use case is the problem, not the product.

Look for repeated words, not single strong opinions.
